    Guide
    

    
    The data
    
    
    
      Every atom has a β, its reduced partition function ratio (RPFR), at
      15 temperatures from 20 to 500 K. A larger β means the site favours the
      heavy isotope (²H, ¹³C, ¹⁵N, ¹⁸O). Values come from DFT harmonic
      frequencies, with translations and rotations removed by Eckart projection.
    
    

    
    Quantities
    
    
    
      
    βthe stored value
    
      
    αβ relative to a reference site
    
      
    1000 ln αα in per mil
    
    
    
    
    
      The reference is the element's mean in this molecule, the dataset average,
      or a site you choose.
    
    

    
    Temperatures
    
    
    
      Stored temperatures are read directly. Others are fitted from ln β against
      1/T, by default with a local quadratic through the four nearest stored
      points. Nothing is stored between 20 K and 60 K, so fits there are unreliable.
    
    

    
    Pictures
    
    
    
      3D: colour is the element; opacity is the magnitude of the RPFR
      relative to that element; labels give the value. 2D: a skeletal
      structure numbered like the table.
    
    
    
    
      If the stored SMILES does not match the geometry, the 2D carries a warning.
      The 3D view always uses the real coordinates.
    
    

    
    Derived
    
    
      
      
    • β against temperature: stored points and the fit between them.
      • 
      
    • Mass-law exponent λ: ¹⁷O against ¹⁸O, for oxygen only.
      • 
      
    • 
        Clumping Δ: the excess of a doubly substituted isotopologue's β
        over the product of the two singly substituted ones, in per mil:
        Δij = 1000 ln(βij / βiβj).
      
      • 
    
    
    
    Mass law and clumping exist only at stored temperatures.
    

    
    Good to know

      • 
      
    • Few molecules have common names. Search by formula, SMILES or group.
      • 
      
    • The 1% subsample holds about 1,300 molecules.
